About HINORY

HINORY puts the people of the Hindu traditions on a map and a timeline at once: sages, acharyas, bhakti poets, yogis, reformers, and the poets, mathematicians, Ayurveda authors and composers who worked inside those traditions. Every figure has a place, a span of years and a note on how firm those years are. Temples, mathas and shrines from every tradition sit on the same map, with links to their own websites where they have one.

How to read the map

● solid
Historical. Dates come from inscriptions, records or the person's own writing.
◐ half
Traditional. A real person whose dates come from hagiography or lineage lists, or are only partly known.
◌ dashed
Legendary. Known from scripture or legend; dates and place are conventional.
◇ diamond
A temple, matha or shrine. Temples are shown in every era; the Temples page lists construction dates where known.
⌒ arc
A guru–disciple link, drawn when both people are on the map.

Sources

Three layers are merged. The first is the hand-built HINORY roster from August 2026, with its notes and temple-board links. The second is a curated list of acharyas, Alvars, Nayanars, Varkari and Haridasa saints, Kerala-school mathematicians and modern teachers. The third was pulled on the date below from Wikidata (CC0) and English Wikipedia: people in the Hindu saint, philosopher, yogi, Ayurveda and Carnatic categories born before 1940 with a known place, and every Hindu temple in South Asia that has a Wikipedia article and coordinates, with its official website from Wikidata or the article's infobox.

Websites

Only a temple's own site, its trust or devasthanam, or the state endowments department page for it is shown. News, travel and blog links were removed. These links come from Wikidata, Wikipedia infoboxes and the original HINORY list. A live reachability check was started on the date below but did not finish, so a few sites may have moved or lapsed.

Limits

Coordinates are town-level, and for people they mark the place most tied to them: a birthplace, a seat or a samadhi. Wikipedia's categories are uneven, so some regions are better covered than others. Imported records keep Wikidata's dates; check a figure's Wikipedia page before quoting a date.
